The problem

Fewer than 1 in 7 psychiatric patients has an advance directive.

Two-thirds say they would make one with help.

The help is the bottleneck. A trained facilitator needs 60 to 90 minutes per person, and clinics don’t have the staff. Without a plan, care defaults to the most aggressive option.

How it works

  1. A guided conversation.

    Built on questions written by dementia and psychiatry clinicians, for the person or their family.

  2. A directive that fits your state’s law.

    Pumpkin drafts it.

  3. A clinician reviews it with you before you sign.

  4. There in a crisis.

    Shared with your family and care team, one quick lookup away, and we track whether it was seen and followed.

Patients never pay.

We start with PACE programs, which receive a fixed payment per older adult and cover all of their care. Every avoided hospital stay is money they keep, and nearly half of PACE participants have some degree of dementia.
