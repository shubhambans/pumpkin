A sample session, start to finish.

Four steps, about a minute. Ruth is made up for this demo, and so are her answers.

A guided conversation.

Built on questions written by dementia and psychiatry clinicians, for the person or their family.

Ruth is 78. This spring she was told she has early Alzheimer’s disease. She sits down with her daughter, Dana, and answers in her own words.

Three sample questions. The real conversation is longer.

When you are frightened or confused in the hospital, what helps most?
If you could no longer recognize your family, what would matter more?
Who should speak for you if you cannot speak for yourself?

Step 1 of 4

A directive that fits your state’s law.

Pumpkin drafts it.

Ruth’s answers become a short document in her own words. Change an answer in step one, or the state here, and the draft follows.

Advance directive, draft

Ruth A.

If I am frightened or confused
Please give me a quiet room and play my own music.
If I can no longer recognize my family
Keep me comfortable, even if that means less time.
Who speaks for me
My daughter, Dana.

Drafted to fit Washington’s rules.

Step 2 of 4

A clinician reviews it with you before you sign.

Dr. Morris, Ruth’s geriatrician, reads the draft back to Ruth and Dana, answers their questions, and checks that it says what Ruth means.

Review with Dr. Morris

  • Read back in plain language
  • Questions answered
  • Ruth confirms these are her wishes

Signed by Ruth A. Reviewed by Dr. Morris.

Step 3 of 4

There in a crisis.

Shared with your family and care team, one quick lookup away, and we track whether it was seen and followed.

Three months later, Ruth arrives at an emergency department at night, confused and on her own.
