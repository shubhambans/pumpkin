For investors

Pumpkin makes advance directives for dementia and serious mental illness that clinicians can find and follow in a crisis. This is the short version of the business.

Two-thirds want a plan. Clinics can’t staff the conversation.

Fewer than 1 in 7 psychiatric patients has an advance directive, and two-thirds say they would make one with help. The help is the bottleneck. A trained facilitator needs 60 to 90 minutes per person, and clinics don’t have the staff. Without a plan, care defaults to the most aggressive option.

Patients never pay. The buyer is the one who keeps the savings.

We start with PACE programs, which receive a fixed payment per older adult and cover all of their care. Every avoided hospital stay is money they keep, and nearly half of PACE participants have some degree of dementia.

Four steps, from a first conversation to a crisis.

  1. A guided conversation.

  2. A directive that fits your state’s law.

  3. A clinician reviews it with you before you sign.

  4. There in a crisis.
